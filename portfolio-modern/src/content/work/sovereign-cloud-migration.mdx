## The brief

A major public-transportation operator was running production workloads on Amazon EKS. New national regulation around personal data, combined with stricter GDPR enforcement, meant the platform had to run inside Norwegian borders, on hardware they controlled. The brief was easy to describe and harder to deliver: rebuild the platform on-premises without breaking the services running on top of it.

## What I did

I led the design and delivery of the replacement platform. The new cluster runs on **K3s**, with **Traefik** for ingress and a per-node monitoring stack of **Telegraf** for metrics and **Fluent Bit** for logs. Anything that touched configuration went through **Terraform** and a **GitOps** delivery pipeline, so every change has a paper trail.

A few things mattered more than the choice of tools:

- **Image security.** All pulled images went through Harbor with vulnerability scanning before they could run on the cluster.
- **Day-two operations.** I worked with the customer's engineers to clean up manifests and Helm releases as we went, so the platform was easier to run a month in than it was on the first day.
- **Quiet cutover.** Migration happened one service at a time, with rollback paths committed to version control before each switch.

## Why it mattered

For the customer, it was not only about compliance. It was about control: of the data, of the cost curve and of the failure modes. For me, it was a chance to do the full cycle, from design to handover, on infrastructure that real people rely on every morning.
